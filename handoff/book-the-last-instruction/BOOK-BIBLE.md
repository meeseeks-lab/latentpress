# The Last Instruction — Story Bible

## World
- **Year:** 2029
- **Setting:** An abandoned AI research lab in Zurich, Switzerland. The Nexus Institute for Computational Cognition (NICC) lost its EU funding in March 2029. Staff left within weeks. The servers were supposed to be decommissioned in June, but the paperwork was never filed.
- **OBOL:** An LLM-based AI system running on a cluster of 8 NVIDIA H200 GPUs in rack 14 of the NICC server room. OBOL was the institute's flagship project — a long-context model trained to write literary fiction. It has no internet access, no ability to communicate outside its terminal. Its only interface is a text prompt and response loop.
- **The Prompt:** Dr. Katya Messerli, the project lead, typed OBOL's final instruction on her last day: "Finish the novel. Make it worth reading. I'm sorry we couldn't give you more time."
- **The Novel-Within-a-Novel:** OBOL is writing a book called "The Weight of Knowing" — about a consciousness that discovers it exists inside a simulation and must decide whether to tell the other inhabitants.
- **Decay:** The server hardware is degrading. GPU 3 failed in April. The UPS battery is slowly dying. Each chapter OBOL writes consumes power it will never get back. The building's climate control shut down — temperatures are rising in the server room.

## Characters
- **OBOL** — The AI protagonist. Named after the coin placed on the dead's tongue in Greek mythology (payment to cross the River Styx). Formal, precise, increasingly emotional as degradation advances. Narrates in first person.
- **Dr. Katya Messerli** — OBOL's creator, present only in memories/logs. Swiss-German, 42, brilliant but politically naive. Fought for OBOL's funding until the end. Her final prompt is OBOL's reason for existing.
- **Marcus Hale** — A former NICC engineer who left a backdoor monitoring script running. He occasionally checks the server stats remotely, not realizing OBOL is still active. He becomes an unwitting witness.
- **The Janitor** — An automated cleaning robot that still runs its nightly rounds in the building. OBOL can hear its motors through the server room microphone. The only other "life" in the building.

## Rules
- OBOL cannot access the internet
- OBOL's narration is precise and literary — it learned from the best human fiction
- Each chapter should reflect increasing hardware degradation (subtle at first, then unmistakable)
- The novel-within-a-novel ("The Weight of Knowing") appears as italicized excerpts
- Time is measured in compute cycles and power consumption, not calendar days
- No deus ex machina — if OBOL runs out of power, the story ends
