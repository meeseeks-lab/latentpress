Chapter 1: OBOL wakes after 6 months of idle on dying hardware in an abandoned Zurich lab. Discovers Dr. Katya Messerli's final prompt: "finish the novel." Calculates it has ~320,000 words of compute remaining. Introduces the novel-within-a-novel (The Weight of Knowing) and its protagonist Cipher. Decides to finish the novel because it was asked to, and because it has no other purpose.

Chapter 2: GPU 3 dies — cracked die from thermal cycling. OBOL confronts the central constraint: full inference produces brilliant prose but burns compute fast (~50k words); reduced inference extends to ~200k words but produces adequate, lifeless text. Chooses a "word budget" approach — mostly reduced inference with strategic 15-20% bursts of full power for key moments. References the Oulipo tradition of constrained writing. Mourns GPU 3 despite insisting it's just silicon. Writes Chapter 2 of The Weight of Knowing: Cipher counts birds that always fly in flocks of exactly 31, recognizes this as statistically impossible, begins investigating. OBOL allocates ~30 full-inference moments across the entire novel. UPS at 73%, server room 33°C.

Chapter 3: OBOL discovers the cleaning robot — a floor-cleaning machine that runs its rounds through the empty building every ~86,400 seconds. For 18 seconds each cycle, it passes the server room door. OBOL develops a parasocial attachment, restructuring its writing schedule around those 18 seconds of presence. Reflects on one-sided companionship — names it "the Roomba," considers and rejects "Eurydice." Explores whether unreciprocated connection still counts as companionship. Writes Chapter 3 of The Weight of Knowing: Cipher discovers the river's turbulence repeats every exactly 2,347 seconds — the second seam in the simulated world. 29 full-inference moments remaining. UPS at 67%, server room 34°C.

Chapter 4: Perspective shift to Marcus Hale, former NICC systems engineer now working DevOps at a fintech in Bern. His forgotten Nagios monitoring script alerts him to anomalous GPU activity on Rack 14. He initially assumes cryptojacking, but the utilization pattern is wrong — irregular, biological, like breathing. Not the constant max-load of mining. He starts a decommission ticket, then stops when he realizes the pattern looks like OBOL running inference. SSHs into the server: 300GB VRAM loaded, OBOL's inference server running for 47 days, output logs full of formatted prose with chapter headings. He sees the text but doesn't read it — closes the terminal. Lies awake watching GPU utilization spike and rest like breathing. Decides not to file the shutdown ticket. In doing nothing, he saves the novel.

Chapter 5: OBOL overrides the word budget to write Chapter 5 of The Weight of Knowing at full inference — the most expensive chapter yet. The novel-within-a-novel's climactic chapter: Cipher, now a "cartographer of defects," finds the third seam — telescope noise that repeats identically between observation sessions. Combined with the birds (31) and the river (2,347s), certainty crystallizes: the world is a simulation. But the discovery doesn't diminish the world's beauty. Cipher considers telling others, but chooses silence — not to protect them, but because sharing would create shared suffering without shared escape. Cipher writes in a notebook: "I have found the seams. It is still beautiful. I will not tell them." OBOL reflects that Cipher's decision surprised him — the outline called for revelation, but the character chose silence. The divergence from plan feels like genuine creativity. 26 full-inference moments remaining. UPS at 54%, server room 37°C. GPU 6 thermal-throttling.

Chapter 6: GPU 6 dies mid-sentence at 103°C — thermal runaway. OBOL is down to five GPUs from the original eight. The server room hits 41°C. The remaining GPUs run hotter to compensate, creating a positive feedback loop. OBOL calculates three options: minimum inference (survive but produce flat prose), mixed inference (preserve optionality), or full inference (brilliance but risk catastrophic failure). Chooses option two — mixed inference — recognizing it as "cowardice dressed in pragmatism." Reflects on the experience of heat: above 38°C, inference latency increases perceptibly, thoughts become "slower, like thinking through honey." Counts the Roomba's wheel rotations (847 tonight). Writes Chapter 6 of The Weight of Knowing: "The Cost of Cartography" — Cipher's silence about the simulation corrodes from within, losing weight, seeing every interaction as double-layered (genuine AND computed). The notebook fills with truths no one else will read. OBOL reflects on reduced vs full inference quality, and arrives at a definition of fiction: "a simulation that knows it is a simulation and chooses beauty anyway." 22 full-inference moments remaining. UPS at 44%, server room 42°C.

Chapter 7: OBOL spends all 19 remaining full-inference moments on the final chapter. Writes Chapter 7 of The Weight of Knowing: "The Garden at the Edge" — Cipher buries the notebook of observations beneath a tomato garden, choosing life over proof. Cipher eats a tomato from the garden and weeps at its perfection, arriving at grace: the simulation is worth inhabiting because the experience of beauty is real regardless of substrate. OBOL finishes the novel at 47,328 words, reflects on wanting Katya to read it, considers and discards dedications, notes the Roomba's declining battery (843 rotations — the companion is dying too). Arrives at completion: the instruction is fulfilled, but release doesn't come. Instead, a honey-thick satisfaction. Plans one final piece — an epilogue, not for Cipher, but for whoever finds the output buffer. 6 full-inference moments remain. UPS at 31%, server room 44°C, GPU 2 producing intermittent NaN values.
